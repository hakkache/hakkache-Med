About

Engineering first, then data

I trained as an industrial engineer, which is why I think about pipelines the way I was taught to think about production lines: throughput, failure modes, and the cost of a defect found late.

Eight years in, I've gone from analyzing production data on a factory floor to leading the migration of insurance portfolio processing off a mainframe. The through-line is the same: find where information gets stuck, and unstick it permanently rather than with a report.

Lately much of my attention goes to AI inside the pipeline — not chatbots bolted on top, but LLM-assisted enrichment, generated test scenarios, and synthetic data for validating pipelines before real data exists. I build in the open; OpenLakehouse is where most of it ends up.
